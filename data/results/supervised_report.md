# Supervised baseline vs Isolation Forest vs a one-line rule

Generated by `python -m src.fraud_detection.supervised --n-splits 5 --n-repeats 5 --seed 42` from the tracked `data/clean/*.csv` and `data/ground_truth_fraud_ids.csv`. Offline; deterministic.

## Setup

- Population: 9370 beneficiaries with >= 1 payment (features from `isolation_forest.build_features`).
- `multivariate_anomaly` (primary): 25 positives (0.27%). `any_planted` (secondary): 1005 positives (10.73%).
- 5-fold stratified CV repeated 5x (seed 42) = 25 test folds; each `multivariate_anomaly` test fold holds only ~5 positives, so one hit moves recall by ~0.20. Cells are `mean ± std [min-max]` over folds.
- A random ranking has PR-AUC ≈ prevalence (0.0027 primary, 0.1073 secondary) and ROC-AUC 0.5.
- `isolation_forest` is fit on the training fold without labels (300 trees, contamination 0.05, seed 42) and scores the test fold. The rule needs no training.
- Budget metrics flag the top 1% / 5% of each test fold; ties at the cut-off are split in expectation.

## Plain-language findings

1. **The planted anomaly is a payment-count artefact.** All 25 planted anomalies have n_payments = 7 (x25); 25 beneficiaries in total have exactly 7. The rule `n_payments > 6` flags 595 beneficiaries, catching 25/25 (precision 0.042). The other flags are not noise: against `any_planted` the same rule has precision 1.000 (recall 0.592), i.e. they are other planted frauds (mostly shared-account victims paid 8-12 times).
2. **With all features the supervised model looks perfect, for a trivial reason.** HGB PR-AUC 1.000 vs Isolation Forest 0.018 vs rule 0.042. The trees learn 'exactly 7 payments', which is how the generator planted them, not a fraud insight: permutation importance puts all of it on n_payments (mean AP drop 0.90; every other feature 0.00).
3. **Where the supervised model does NOT beat the rule (primary target).** Logistic regression is monotone in n_payments, so it cannot express 'exactly 7' and only ties the rule: PR-AUC 0.047 vs rule 0.042, and recall at the 5% budget 0.78 vs rule 0.80. Isolation Forest is below the rule on every metric and every feature set.
4. **Dropping n_payments alone hides little**, because total_amount = mean_amount x n_payments still encodes it: HGB PR-AUC 0.813, with total_amount now the top feature (AP drop 0.80).
5. **Without count and total, what remains is weak and unstable.** PR-AUC: HGB 0.292 (fold range 0.01-0.61), logreg 0.045, Isolation Forest 0.019 (random ≈ 0.0027). HGB ranks a few anomalies very high (precision at 1% 0.086) but at the 5% budget it recalls only 0.42 -- LESS than the rule (0.80) and less than logreg (0.65). The signal left is mean_amount and amount_to_income (planted anomalies get higher amounts on lower incomes), real but not enough to find them at a usable precision; with ~5 positives per fold the spread is wide.
6. **Secondary target (any planted fraud): no gain where it matters.** PR-AUC rule 0.636, HGB (all) 0.689, logreg (all) 0.672, Isolation Forest (all) 0.539. At the 1% and 5% budgets HGB and the rule are identical (precision 1.00 vs 1.00, recall at 5% 0.468 vs 0.468): both just pick high payment counts. The small PR-AUC edge comes from ordering the rest of the list. Without count/total HGB drops to 0.375. Identity fraud is invisible to these features and is the job of the rule-based and fuzzy detectors.

Bottom line: on this synthetic data the supervised model's edge is the generator's fingerprint (exactly 7 payments). It is a ceiling for these features, not evidence that a supervised model would work on real cases, where labels are scarce and fraud patterns move.

## Leakage guard

Features are built by `isolation_forest.build_features` before the answer key is read; labels are attached afterwards as `y_*` columns. `assert_no_leakage` runs on every feature set and rejects answer-key columns, `y_*` labels, identifiers (`row_id`, `beneficiary_id`) and anything outside `FEATURE_COLUMNS`. Pinned by `tests/test_supervised.py`.

## Caveats

- 25 positives: fold-level numbers move in steps of ~0.2 recall; trust the spread, not the third decimal.
- The CSV has no DB `row_id`; file order stands in for it (only used to pick one row per duplicated beneficiary_id). Results can differ slightly from the DB-backed Isolation Forest run.
- `any_planted` labels only beneficiaries named in the answer key; hub-account owners are not listed there and count as negatives.
